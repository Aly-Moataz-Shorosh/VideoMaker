## 1. The core (M1 EP1)

**Intentional vs non-intentional content.** ~90% of a feed is non-intentional (humour, attractiveness, random luck, no clear
purpose, uncontrolled variables, inconsistent views). ~10% is intentional: made with a clear purpose, controlled variables,
A/B testing, iterated. Signs of intentional accounts: clear demonstration, a consistent hook / way of talking, a consistent
format and filming style, one niche, they link you somewhere / sell something. Signs of non-intentional: too short or random,
purely topical (watch and forget), brain-rot, relies on something rare (a pretty face, a cool car), views jump up and down.

**Views ≠ sales.** Course case: a plain service-announcement video, 40,000 views, 9% conversion, ~4.5 M EGP revenue vs a viral
«masry ye2ool ma3rafsh» video, 8.7 M views, 0% conversion, 0 EGP. Not every video is made to get views.

**The 3 points of view.** Viewer wants stimulation · Creator wants money (sales / leads) · Platform wants session time (ads).
A video works when it serves all three.

**Video life cycle (course model).** The platform shows a video to a small batch, scores the interactions, and if it passes a
threshold it pushes it to a bigger batch; every level's threshold is higher (pass, then push). Course scoring model for short
form: like 1 · comment 2 · share 3 · full watch 4 · rewatch 5. The two heaviest signals are WATCH signals (full watch, rewatch),
then share. So we optimise for: finished watches and rewatches first, shares second, comments third, likes last.

**Watch decision ≠ purchase decision.** I watch because I am stimulated; I buy because I see added value. The perfect video does
both; most videos lean to one side, so you need a mix (the funnel).

**Why most accounts fail to monetise:** 1) entertainment-only approach (views over everything), 2) they see content from one
point of view only, 3) they post for the sake of posting (no control, schedule or direction).

**For Aly:** his videos are intentional by nature (series, styles, recipes). The risk is the opposite one: deep, useful videos
that nobody is stimulated to start. Every script needs a stimulation layer (hook, curiosity, visual) on top of the value.

## 2. The funnel (M1 EP2)

| | TYPE A = TOFU | TYPE B = MOFU | TYPE C = BOFU |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job | get attention / awareness | build trust | drive action / conversion |
| Viewer | doesn't know or care about you | knows you, slightly interested | trusts you, considering action |
| Nature | entertainment-driven, zero context needed | value-driven, some interest needed | proof-driven, high intent |
| Formats | skits, day-in-the-life, interviews, challenges, trends, tier lists | how-to, breakdowns, storytelling, founder journey, behind the scenes | testimonials, case studies, demos, offers |
| Length / pace | short, fast, low friction | medium-longer, structured | focused, efficient |
| Messaging | broad, relatable, simple | clear, insightful, a bit deeper | direct, specific, persuasive |
| CTA | rare / none | light, implied | strong and clear |
| Emotion | curiosity, entertainment, relatability | trust, credibility, connection | confidence, urgency, certainty |
| Metrics | views, watch time, shares | saves, replays | leads, messages, conversions |
| Success sounds like | «this went viral» | «this helped me» | «I want this / I bought this» |
| Done wrong | high views, zero business impact | smart content, no authority | pushy, low trust, no sales |

**When to push each type (re-decide quarterly):**
- More A: starting from zero, low or stuck reach, new market/audience, rebrand/pivot.
- More B: views but weak engagement quality, people watch but don't follow or remember you, known but not respected yet.
- More C: steady audience + engagement, launching/selling, want to monetise existing attention.

**El ESMA (the split / «القسمة»)** — course examples: 50/40/10, 40/40/20, 65/35/0, 0/70/30. Homus report bands:
new business 60/30/10 · growing 45/35/20 · established 30/40/30 · offer-heavy 25/35/40.
**The content calendar** spreads the letters across the week so the same type rarely sits back to back (A B A C A B …), never
a block of C.

**Aly's own ESMA (his Homus report, answers: online course/education, Cairo, budget price):** 55% A / 35% B / 10% C.
Report diagnosis: content health 31/100, biggest bottleneck = AWARENESS (not enough new people find him), posting not consistent,
offer not validated. Fix first = reach: strong-hook shorts, common mistakes, myth-busting, relatable problems, quick tips.
At 10 videos/week that is 6 A / 3 B / 1 C; at 5/week ≈ 3 A / 2 B / (1 C every other week).

**What A / B / C mean for Aly** (he sells attention, his courses, his freelance craft):
- A (reach): shorts that need zero context — Egyptian identity and history surprises («مصر»), AI before/after shock, myths, mistakes,
  rankings/tier lists, «هتصدقني لو قلتلك…», trend reels (style 19), event moments (style 26), cinema remixes.
- B (trust): tutorials and course shorts, explainers (هو ايه), behind-the-scenes («الـ AI مش ساحر»: how a video was really made,
  versions, costs), tests (اختبرتلك), انت بتشتغل ايه episodes, long YouTube videos in general.
- C (action): the CTA layer — «the full course / full video» with thumbnail + QR, Instagram keyword → DM resource, his Photopea course,
  his services / Behance portfolio as proof, real results of his students/clients, sponsor-ready proof. Conversion for him = subscribe,
  watch the long video, comment the keyword, DM for work.

## 3. Research (M2 EP1)

**The 6-step process:** research → ideation → scripting → filming → editing/posting → optimizing (then back to research).
**80/20:** 20% of inputs give 80% of results. What decides performance (course model): idea 40% · script 40% · quality+editing 20%.
Most creators spend their week the wrong way round (filming 35% + editing 35%, only ~30% of the work done before filming);
Homus wants ~75% done before filming: research 20% · ideation 15% · scripting 40% · filming 10% · editing 15%.
**For Aly:** he over-invests in editing/motion (his strength). The method says the IDEA and the SCRIPT carry 80% — so the time
we save with the motion library must go into research + script, not into more effects.

**Research = build a reference bank of ≥ 60 strong videos** that already performed, of different kinds (educational, story, demo,
transformation, comparison, mistakes, behind the scenes, opinion, customer story, entertainment). Rules:
- Don't search only your exact niche; go one level broader and into adjacent industries with similar audiences, problems,
  transformations or content styles (a dentist studies doctors + skincare; an accountant studies finance educators + lawyers).
- Search the PROBLEM, the desire, the mistake, the fear, the misunderstanding — not only the product.
- Searches are 2-5 simple words, the way normal people type.
- Prioritise performance over perfect relevance: a strong video from any niche teaches how to open, create curiosity, explain,
  show a transformation, tell a story, demo, build trust, sell without feeling like an ad.
- Don't judge a search after 2-3 videos; scroll. Save videos that make you ask «why did this work?». Choose by EVIDENCE that the
  audience liked it (high views / likes / comments vs the account's normal), not by personal taste.
- Copy the STRUCTURE, never the content: «3 things I would never do as a dermatologist» → «3 things I would never do as a mechanic».

**Search map** (the «Updated prompt»): A) 5 broad industry searches, B) 5 customer-problem searches, C) 5 customer-desire searches,
D) 3 adjacent industries × 3 searches + why each helps, E) 10 format searches (before/after, mistakes, beginner guide, things nobody
tells you, behind the scenes, how it's made, reaction, comparison, transformation, myth vs fact, customer story, process,
experiment, ranking, storytime), F) 5 creator TYPES (never invent named creators), then the TOP 15 searches ranked by: chance of many
videos, chance of high performers, usefulness, ease. If the niche is hard: niche → broader category → similar industries →
similar audience problems → similar formats.

**Per-creator analysis sheet** (top 5 videos of a creator): link, views, likes, comments, SHARES ★, SAVES ★ (the VIP metrics), length;
hook (first sentence + type visual/verbal/text/mix), angle + sub-angle, format, pacing, setting, lighting, props/B-roll, archetype A/B/C,
CTA yes/no, caption style, hashtag strategy, cover frame; then the THINKING LAYER: why did it perform (hook timing? emotion?
contrarian? relatable moment? algorithm? a viral comment? a trend?) and what would you do differently / what can you ethically steal.
Optional fields: emotional trigger (fear / curiosity / humour / awe / anger / aspiration — the strongest predictor of shares),
comment sentiment (many questions = curiosity loop worth repeating), rewatch potential 1-5, trend vs evergreen (aim ~20/80),
sound strategy, creator tier (micro 10K-100K / mid / macro 1M+ — don't copy a macro when you're micro), content pillar.
**Synthesis sheet:** top 3 patterns across creators, most common hook, most effective angle, what I'll copy, what I'll avoid,
the GAP nobody fills, my unique angle, first 3 videos, hypotheses to test.
**Video bank sheet:** link, views, likes, shares, saves, length, FIRST 3 SECONDS, why I stopped (curiosity / relatable / funny /
useful / controversial / strong visual / bold claim / storytelling / fast pacing / surprise), idea I can use, STRONG flag when
share rate or save rate ≥ 1% of views.

**Lessons from the filled research (Mexican food in Cairo):**
- Controversy + Egyptian identity = virality. The best hook was not about the product, it was about US («Egyptian streets finally got
  real tacos», «I put tahini inside a taco»). Fusion of the foreign and the familiar gets shared («this is OUR thing now»).
- Family/ordinary-person reaction («my Egyptian mum tried … for the first time») = high relatability, easy share.
- «Brutally honest» rankings/reviews build trust fast because they are critical, not only positive.
- «Secret ingredient / this one thing makes it 10x better» hooks win; voice-over without a face can work if pacing is fast.
- Shorter videos had 3-4x the save rate; over 60 s needs a clear story arc to justify it.
- A thinking creator (fewer videos, a clear angle each) beats a producing creator (many videos, no point of view).
- Losers: «welcome to our brand» first video (nobody cares who you are yet), step-by-step without a human story («step by step» signals
  effort the viewer didn't ask for), a Wikipedia-headline informational video («why X is healthy»), announcements for small accounts
  (reframe as a story: «we almost didn't open this branch — here's why»), text slides + voice-over with no face/story/tension,
  oversaturated slow day-in-the-life, product reveals that feel like ads, slow Q&A with weak questions.
**For Aly:** «مصر» identity, Egyptian cinema × world remixes and «AI in Egyptian hands» are exactly the controversy+identity+fusion
pattern. Use it deliberately in A-type shorts.

## 4. Ideation (M2 EP2) — «find what works and make it yours»

Of all ideas on social media ~95% are «repeated» ideas, ~3% bad new ideas, ~2% good new ideas (which get stolen). So: don't chase new
ideas; take proven FORMATS and make them yours. Series ideas always win.

**FORMAT ≠ TOPIC.** Format = the shape («3 mistakes», «before vs after», «rating things», «day in the life», «POV», «reacting to comments»,
«things I wish I knew», «myth vs fact», «things X won't tell you», «real numbers / cost breakdown», «real vs fake / how to spot»,
«phrase/term of the day», «X vs Y», «ranking / tier list», «what it really costs»). Topic = what it is about.

**The Content Factory (worksheet):** 1) pick a saved reference, write what happens in one sentence, split format/topic →
2) remove the topic, keep the format with a blank (×5) → 3) drop your topic into the blank (×5) → 4) one format → 5 videos
(same format, new angle) for 3 formats → 5) SERIES TEST: can it make 5 (the answer key says 10) episodes? yes = keep as a series,
no = delete (one-off) → 6) content bank: 3 series × 5 episodes = 15+ ideas from 3 references. Rerun whenever ideas run out.

**Course idea lists show the pattern** (toothpaste: be a doctor / before-after / recipes / «best for sensitivity, whitening, kids,
braces» / pain → solution; Hyrox: «explained for beginners», «X vs Y difference», «which burns more», «how to prepare for your first»;
suit shop: «Suits 101» series, «2,000 vs 10,000 EGP suit», «road to find affordable suits», «stop sweating in suits», glow-up).
Patterns: 101-series, X vs Y, cheap vs expensive with real prices, beginner's first-time guide, myths, mistakes, local («in Cairo»).

**Worked-case lessons (10 businesses):** easy niches are visual/emotional; «boring» niches win with shock + satisfying before/after;
high-ticket low-trust niches need more B (real numbers, myths, «what salesmen won't tell you»); the ESMA moves with the business
(new + impulse → A-heavy 70-75%; high-ticket/trust → 40/40/20 or 45/35/20).

**For Aly — formats that fit his topics:** «هو ايه» 101-series, X vs Y (DaVinci vs Premiere, Veo vs Kling), cheap vs expensive with real
EGP prices (his bills), «اختبرتلك» real numbers, myths in AI/graphics/history, «3 mistakes beginners make in …», «things nobody tells you
about freelancing», «how it was really made» (behind the AI video), «rating / tier list» (AI tools, Egyptian films' VFX), «هتصدقني لو
قلتلك…» history/geography surprises, «real vs fake / AI or real?» quizzes, before/after restoration & colorization, «reacting to comments».

## 5. Scripting (M2 EP3) — the attention game

A script has 3 parts with 3 jobs: HOOK grabs attention · BODY keeps attention · CTA directs attention.
**Visual > text > verbal** — the brain sees before it hears (light is faster than sound). The first frame must already work with the
sound off; then on-screen text; then the spoken line.

**The hook is a PROMISE** that the video is worth the time, said EXPLICITLY (directly: «تعالى أقولك إزاي …») or IMPLICITLY (indirectly:
a result, a tension, a strange image). Test hooks with the PAUSE TEST on your best references: pause after the hook — do you need to
know what comes next?

**Bad → better → best (the course's 5 cases, paraphrased):**
1. Low-calorie sauce. Bad: greetings + «I own a taco place, let me show you how we make the sauce». Better: «our most famous sauce, made
   in 7 minutes, let me show you». Best: «this sauce is always sold out, 200 calories a serving, made in 5 minutes» = three promises
   at once (taste/proof, healthy, easy).
2. Day in the life. Bad: «Hi guys, I'm the brand owner, come with me». Better: «today was one of the hardest days of my life as a
   business owner, let me show you». Best: 7-10 fast clips foreshadowing the whole day up to its climax + a line that frames it.
3. Hyrox glutes. Bad: «let me talk about the importance of glutes». Better: «you've been running wrong your whole life». Best: «how I
   cut my time from X to Y and my weight from X to Y in one month with just this move» (better time + weight + simple).
4. Stocks vs bonds. Bad: «what's the difference?». Better: «would you believe you can own part of Apple — and lend it money too?».
   Best: «how the richest people make their money and how you can do the same in 30 seconds» (knowledge + action).
5. Plaque. Bad: «let me tell you about plaque». Better: «why plaque matters for clean teeth». Best: «this one thing may be why your breath
   smells and you don't even know» (simple thing + unpopular thing).
Pattern: the best hook stacks 2-3 concrete promises, uses a specific number or a personal stake, and talks about the viewer's result,
not the creator.

**The body can do 3 things with the promise:** KEEP it (deliver), ONE-UP it (over-deliver: «and on top of that it costs under 4 EGP»),
or make a NEW promise (extend: «but that wasn't the only lesson that day — then my friend said let's go skydiving»). Body examples:
«but it's not only that — many people also suffer from …», «but you'll never invest right unless you understand …», «but the glutes
alone never work without the hamstrings — let me show you how to stretch them right», «and honestly, that's not our best seller…».
**Don't lose them:** PACING (every point leads to the next; smooth flow between sentences/clips), REDUNDANCY (never say/show the same
thing twice; remove anything that adds no value), STIMULATION (always a reason to stay; they always wonder what's next).

**CTA — two kinds:** FOLLOWS (grow the audience) or LEADS (start a conversation / sale). Follow-CTA templates (Arabic, fill the blanks):
- «نفسك تنتقل من [الوضع الحالي] لـ [النتيجة اللي بتحلم بيها]؟ اعمل Follow عشان تتعلم الخطوات اللي هتوصلك.»
- «عايز توصل لنفس النتيجة اللي وصلها [الشخص/العميل]؟ اعمل Follow لمعلومات أكتر.»
- «بتعاني من [المشكلة]؟ اعمل Follow لمعلومات أكتر تساعدك توصل لـ [النتيجة].»
- «نفسك حد يختصر عليك سنين التجارب والأخطاء؟ اعمل Follow وأنا هوصلك لـ [النتيجة] أسرع.»
- «مهتم بـ [المجال]؟ اعمل Follow لمعلومات أكتر.» — and the simplest: «Follow because I share all the secrets».
Optimization cases add: a CTA that asks a SPECIFIC comment («what salary should I calculate next?», «comment your goal: lose fat or build
muscle», «comment the sentence you want in English») beats «follow for more»; «send this to someone with a small living room» drives shares;
«save this for …» drives saves; «book now» is too early for cold viewers.

The Scripting Techniques Library (22 techniques) is in `techniques.md`. Quick template: core idea (one sentence: who, result, method) ·
hook (2-3 versions, pick one) · key points in delivery order · micro-hooks (where you reset or layer curiosity) · proof (where) · CTA
(one line, only after the final point).

## 6. Filming & editing (only the 4 CapCut screenshots)

What the timelines show (2:09, 0:55, 0:52, 0:24 vertical talking-head videos):
- Talking head cut every ~1.5-3 s (50+ clips in 2 minutes): every pause, breath and restart is gone.
- Location/background changes between sentences (compound clips shot in 4-5 places in a 24 s video) = a pattern interrupt per sentence.
- «Steps» videos: a text label per step («أربع خطوات تساعدك…» → Step 1 … Step 4) with a «ding / bell» sfx on every label;
  B-roll pictures/screenshots on the words that name them; a stack of ~8 quick stickers/icons popping together for a list;
  a red highlight bar; speed ramps and «special effect» clips stacked at the hook; whoosh + layered dings at the opening.
This matches what we already do (sfx per label, picture on its word, cut on waveform). Their density is the target for A-type shorts.

## 7. The feedback loop (M2 EP5) — unlocked after 10 posts

- Wrong mental model: research → script → film → edit → post → done. Right: … → post → OPTIMIZE → research again. The output of
  optimization is the input of research (if informational videos do well, or a topic / format / person / product does, research THAT).
- It is an «information creation» course: information → patterns → systems → results → new information, forever.
- The market is your teacher; «رأيك في طز» — your opinion of an idea doesn't matter, only a test does (ideas they hated worked, ideas they
  loved failed). Only way to know = try it.
- Every niche/market has its methods: F&B = tasty food + employee content; services = testimony + case studies; startups = founder-led
  storytelling; female audience = gossip, aesthetics; what works abroad doesn't automatically work here (and the reverse).
- RATIOS > NUMBERS: 1,000 saves on 5,000 likes = good; on 50,000 likes = bad. High comment ratio = interactive/controversial; low =
  forgettable. High share ratio = people send it to friends; low = funny but not relatable.
- Every number is feedback — its presence AND its absence. Comments are market research: one comment can become a hook, a video, a series,
  or an offer improvement.
- Outliers: in a batch of 15 (1 at 200K, 2-13 at 10-50K, 14 at 2K): the 200K is obviously good (repeat it), the 2K obviously bad (drop it,
  don't let it kill you), the middle is where you apply the method. Luck or not? Make a hypothesis and test it («حط الكارت في جيبك»):
  observe → hypothesize → test → repeat.
